Protein Function Retrieval

Predicting the value of structure replacement

Replacing a structural representation does not guarantee better retrieval. This project examines which proteins benefit from replacement and compares prediction quality with the retrieval gains achieved after selection.

Key findings

Gains are concentrated in a minority of proteins.

37 of the 297 eligible test proteins produced a positive change in macro nDCG@10 when replaced individually.

Prediction quality and retrieval gain are different outcomes.

The random forest achieved an AP of 0.4058. At a 10% replacement budget, its retrieval gain was +0.0032, compared with +0.0139 for the query ranking heuristic.

A larger budget does not guarantee a larger gain.

For the query ranking heuristic, the gain decreased from +0.0139 at a 10% budget to +0.0006 at a 20% budget.

These are test-set point estimates for the primary analysis. Intervals and evaluation conditions are provided below; the results do not establish universal superiority of any strategy.

What is being replaced?

Replacement switches a protein's structural score from ProstT5-generated 3Di to 3Di extracted from an AlphaFold DB structure. The term “upgrade” describes this operation; it does not assume that retrieval or structural quality must improve.

Methods and scope

Methods

The study uses public CAFA5 sequences and time-delayed function annotations. Proteins are assigned to training, validation, and test sets by sequence-homology cluster. ProTrek encodes sequences, structural tokens, and function text. The primary analysis uses a sequence fusion weight of 0.10, selected through the existing validation procedure.

Gain prediction uses 40 features available before replacement. Hyperparameters and score directions are chosen on validation data. The course analysis recomputes results on the previously used test split; it is not a new independent validation.

Evaluation labels

Known positives and annotation pairs overlapping the old CAFA5 training labels are excluded from query-specific scoring. Unlabeled proteins remain in the ranking, but missing annotations do not establish biological negatives.

Scope of the findings

Queries were selected partly using their test-set positive counts, so the findings apply to this selected set of 28 queries. Gain also depends on the candidate library and selection context.

Existing AlphaFold DB structures are used here. Replacement budgets are count-based approximations and do not establish savings in structure-prediction time or cost.

Glossary

GO molecular function
A standardized description of a protein's molecular activity.
3Di
A structural alphabet representing local three-dimensional relationships.
nDCG@10
A ranking metric that rewards relevant proteins appearing near the top of the first ten results.
AP
Average precision for ranking proteins with positive replacement gains; distinct from retrieval MAP@10.
Macro average
An equally weighted average over scorable queries.
Replacement budget
The number of eligible proteins that may switch structural representations.
